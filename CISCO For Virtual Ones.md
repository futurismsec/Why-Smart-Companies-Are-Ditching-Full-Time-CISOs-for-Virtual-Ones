# Why-Smart-Companies-Are-Ditching-Full-Time-CISOs-for-Virtual-Ones
Why fractional CISOs are becoming the smarter, leaner bet for growing businesses
There's a shift happening in how companies staff their security leadership, and it's not the one most people expected.

For a long time, the playbook was simple: if security matters to your business and it always does you hire a full-time CISO. Permanent seat at the table, permanent line in the budget, permanent headcount. That was just how it was done.

A growing number of companies are quietly breaking from that playbook. Instead of hiring a full-time Chief Information Security Officer, they're bringing in a Virtual CISO (vCISO)  fractional, flexible, and scoped to what the business actually needs.

This isn't a budget workaround for companies that can't afford "the real thing." For a lot of organizations, it's turning out to be the better model, period.

The math on full-time CISOs stopped adding up

A full-time CISO hire isn't just a salary line. Once you factor in base pay (often north of $250K), bonuses, equity, benefits, and the team they'll eventually want to build, you're looking at a serious long-term commitment one built around a single person's calendar.

And that person doesn't stay put. Security leadership has one of the highest turnover rates in the executive suite, because good CISOs get recruited aggressively and often leave within two years. Every time that happens, the company loses institutional knowledge and has to restart the hiring process, usually while its security program is mid-stride.

On top of that, most mid-sized companies don't have a steady 40-hours-a-week workload of executive-level security decisions. They need someone thinking strategically about risk, compliance, and governance but not necessarily every single day, every single week, forever.

Full-time hiring assumes a flat, constant need. Real security work isn't flat. It spikes around audits, funding rounds, incidents, and compliance deadlines, then levels off.

What a fractional model actually solves

A vCISO engagement is built around that reality instead of ignoring it.

You get:

Program development security frameworks mapped to standards like NIST, ISO 27001, or CIS Controls, built for your actual risk profile instead of a generic template
Compliance ownership someone who actually owns HIPAA, GDPR, PCI DSS, or SOX requirements instead of it being an afterthought bolted onto IT
Governance and reporting board-ready updates that translate technical risk into business language
Strategic road mapping multi-year planning that lines up with where the business is actually headed
Flexible intensity more support during an audit or acquisition, lighter touch once things stabilize

None of that requires a full-time seat. It requires senior judgment, applied when and where it's needed.

The quiet inefficiency nobody talks about

Here's the part that doesn't get said out loud enough: a lot of full-time CISOs end up underutilized. Once the initial security program is built out, the ongoing strategic workload often doesn't fill a full week, but the company is still paying full executive compensation for it.

A fractional model closes that gap. You're paying for expertise and judgment, not for a seat being occupied five days a week regardless of actual workload.

Who this model fits best

A few situations come up again and again:

First-time security leadership. Companies that have never had a dedicated security executive can establish the function without committing to a six-figure hire before they even know what they need.

M&A activity. Security due diligence during a merger or acquisition needs to move fast and be right — a fractional CISO can step in for exactly that window.

Regulated industries. Healthcare, finance, and retail all carry compliance obligations that shift constantly. Someone needs to own that full-time attention without necessarily being a full-time hire.

Leadership transitions. When a CISO leaves, a fractional replacement keeps the security program moving instead of stalling out during a months-long search.

Fast-growing companies. Rapid scaling tends to outpace security maturity. A fractional CISO helps keep that gap from becoming a real liability.

What actually matters when evaluating one

Not every fractional CISO offering is built the same, so a few things worth checking before committing:

Real prior experience running a security program not just advising around the edges of one
Industry-specific context, since healthcare security and manufacturing security are genuinely different problems
Recognized certifications (CISSP, CISM, CRISC) as a baseline signal of competence
Boardroom fluency the ability to explain risk in terms executives and non-technical stakeholders actually understand
Scalability, since your needs today won't match your needs in eighteen months

If any of those are missing, the engagement won't deliver the strategic value it's supposed to.

The bigger picture

This shift isn't really about cutting costs, even though the savings are real. It's a recognition that security leadership doesn't have to be all-or-nothing. Companies can get executive-level strategy, governance, and compliance oversight without permanently expanding the C-suite around it.
